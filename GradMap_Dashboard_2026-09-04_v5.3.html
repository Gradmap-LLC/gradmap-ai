Careers & Majors

Work backwards, not forwards. Most students pick a major and hope a career comes out of it. This does it the other way round.
1

Start wide, then cut

You cannot narrow down something you have never seen. Look at a broad set of careers and remove the ones that are clearly not you, until 10–15 are left.

›
2

Work back to majors

Ask which majors actually lead to those careers. Look for the ones that serve several at once. That is your 2–3, and it keeps your options open.

›
3

Test them with activities

Pick activities that let you find out whether you actually like the major. Your resume is what falls out of doing that honestly.

Why we do it this way A major is a route, not a destination, and it is much easier to say "not that" than "this one." Eliminating careers you do not want gets you somewhere real in an afternoon, where staring at a list of 300 majors gets you nowhere in a week. Doing it in this order also means every activity on your application has a reason behind it, which is exactly what an admissions reader is looking for.

1. What pulls you?

Pick at least three. Not subjects, things you like doing.

2. Careers

Sorted by what you just said. Cut the ones that are not you.

3. Majors

4. Activities that test them

Not resume padding. A way to find out.
This is the CPSI method, not a quiz. Nothing here predicts anything or tells a student what to be. It narrows honestly, shows why each step follows the last, and ends with something they can actually go and do this month. What they keep writes to Major Interests in their profile; the activities they add land in My Plan.
